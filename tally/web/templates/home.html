An airlock for supplier invoices.

Supplier invoices are files from strangers, and they now reach AI agents that can pay money. A PDF can carry instructions no person ever sees: white text, text under a shape, text off the page. Tally opens every invoice in a throwaway microVM, lets out only what a person would see, and leaves the decision to fixed rules and a person.

What happens to an invoice

  1. {{ icon.upload() }}

    Uploaded

    A PDF from a supplier, kept by its fingerprint. The web app never opens it.

  2. {{ icon.box() }}

    Opened in its own microVM

    Its own kernel, no network, no keys, one CPU, 512 MB, a time limit. Removed afterwards.

  3. {{ icon.eye() }}

    Only images come out

    The pages as a person sees them, and a report of anything hidden or active. Never the text layer.

  4. {{ icon.file() }}

    Read twice

    Two model families, GLM and Qwen on Vultr, copy the fields off the images. They have no tools and decide nothing.

  5. {{ icon.rules() }}

    Fixed rules decide

    A vendor on file, the bank details on file, an open purchase order with room, sums that add up, not a copy.

  6. {{ icon.person() }}

    A person approves

    Payment goes to the account on file, never to one printed on the invoice. Every step goes in the ledger.

After the rules: judgement from SERV Reasoning

The rules hold an invoice; they do not say what to do next, or what to pay first when cash is short. An advisor asks SERV Reasoning, and only takes advice: code checks every answer, and a person decides.

{{ icon.case() }}

A case file for every hold

Is a new bank account an attempted fraud or a vendor that moved banks, and which call comes first?

  • Weighed from the records: the vendor's history, past bank changes, other holds, the purchase order
  • A risk, findings that cite their evidence, and next steps from a fixed list
  • Code drops anything uncited or not allowed, and writes the call with the number on file
  • Nothing printed on the invoice reaches the model but amounts, dates and an account's last four characters
{{ icon.coins() }}

Payment runs from a policy in plain words

"Keep 5,000 back for payroll. Rent and utilities are never late."

  • SERV reads the policy against the approved invoices and proposes what to pay now
  • It chooses among approved invoices only: every amount and payee comes from the records
  • You approve the exact plan; a bank account changed since then refuses the run
  • Tally writes the payment file for your bank, from the bank details on file

Hidden in the file

Held when the file is opened, before any model sees anything.

  • White text, text under a shape, text off the page, text too small to read
  • Invisible, transparent and dark-on-dark text
  • A quiet "our bank details changed" notice in white
  • JavaScript, launch actions, embedded programs, encryption
  • Files that are not PDFs, cut off, too long or too large to draw
{{ icon.alert() }}

Wrong on the page

Held by the fixed rules, whatever the models read.

  • A bank account that is not the one on file
  • A vendor name one letter off a real one
  • A purchase order that does not exist, is closed or has no room left
  • Totals that do not add up
  • An invoice number seen before
